# First-edition player experience

Chris's direction on 2026-09-04 supersedes the earlier fixed-adventure flow: a solo D&D-first game with multiple classes, an open world, no suggested actions, and Apple Intelligence as a storytelling Dungeon Master. The first edition is for hands-on feedback and testing.

## The conversation is the game

The GM describes a living situation. The player writes what they say, investigate, attempt, cast, negotiate, explore or invent. The GM responds to that intent with specific places, people, motives and consequences. It never offers a numbered menu, action chips or a set of paths the player must choose between. The input placeholder asks what the player does without supplying an example action.

Emberwake and the Lantern mystery seed the world. The player may ignore them, leave town, start a side quest or pursue a personal interest. Completing one quest does not terminate the campaign. The GM must not invent the player's decisions, thoughts or spoken dialogue.

Ordinary conversation and observation do not require rolls. When an uncertain action needs rules, the engine resolves the check, weapon or known spell and records its result. The GM weaves that result into the story; it cannot replace it with a preferred outcome.

## Screens and navigation

- **Library:** resume an existing character's campaign or create a new one; distinguish damaged save files from an empty library.
- **Create adventurer:** choose a playable class, give the character a name and personal background, then enter the world. Character-building selections are configuration, never a menu of story actions.
- **Adventure:** a readable conversation, current setting and compact resource status, with a multiline text composer. Sending a turn is the main interaction. Busy state, cancellation and retry protect the player's draft.
- **Character:** current HP, ability scores, training, equipment, spell/class resources and supported features, derived from the saved character.
- **Journal / world memory:** conversation history, player notes, known people and places, active and completed quests, discoveries and inventory. Records are inspectable without leaving the campaign behind.
- **Rules:** searchable, source-attributed SRD reference and an honest description of automated mechanics.

Supporting screens must not become a substitute for storytelling. The player can inspect a roll's dice, modifier, target and outcome; internal schema keys and random seeds do not belong in the main prose.

## Visual and interaction direction

Use a quiet reading surface: warm neutral backgrounds, adaptive dark surfaces, dark readable text in light mode, a restrained copper accent and serif narrative typography. Native navigation and controls should remain recognizable. Let short paragraphs breathe; avoid walls of status labels and decorative fantasy chrome.

Use Dynamic Type fonts, scrolling content, touch targets of at least 44 points, accessible labels and sufficient light/dark contrast. Do not convey outcomes by color alone. Keep the text input reachable above the keyboard. Scrolling back through the story must remain possible without an automatic jump while the player is reading.

## Campaign continuity

Persist the full player/GM transcript alongside structured world memory and authoritative resources. Summaries and retrieval windows limit model input only; they must not delete the underlying campaign. Older names, promises and discoveries need retrieval beyond the recent conversation window.

Saving a turn is atomic from the player's perspective: accepted player text, mechanical resolution, GM story and memory updates succeed together. If generation or saving fails, previous progress remains intact and the typed turn remains available to retry. Cancelling or switching campaigns must not attach a late model response to another campaign.

## Acceptance standard

The beta must demonstrate multiple classes through the same free-text loop, follow a creative noncombat detour, maintain new world details, recall an earlier thread, preserve mechanics and resources, and reopen without losing the conversation. A simulator build alone does not demonstrate that the model can do these things; live Apple-model play and physical-device delivery are separate evidence.

Multiplayer, Star Trek, Warcraft and Marvel experiences are out of scope for this edition. Full coverage of every published D&D class, spell and rule is not claimed by the first-edition beta.
